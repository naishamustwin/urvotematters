Before you vote, know what’s on your ballot.

Candidates aren’t the only decisions you’ll make when you vote. Your ballot may include local races, judges, amendments, referendums, constitutional questions, school boards, and other issues that directly affect your community.

Democracy works when we protect the process.

You don’t have to be a political expert to care about how elections are administered. Your state and local election officials are responsible for making sure elections are conducted fairly, securely, and transparently.

Find my Secretary of State (opens NASS in a new tab)

Opens the NASS member directory. In some states, elections are run by a lieutenant governor, a state board, or an elections commission instead. Choose your state above for a direct link to your official state election office.

Send this to your Secretary of State

Or to your state’s chief election official. Copy it, then paste it into their contact form or an email.

Subject: Protect Our Election Process

I am a voter who cares about the integrity, security, accessibility, and transparency of our elections. Please continue providing clear information about election procedures, voter registration, ballot access, election security, and how voters can verify official election information.

I believe voters should have accurate information and confidence in the process. Thank you for your work protecting our elections and serving voters in our state.

Don’t let fear make your voting decisions for you.

Political information moves faster than facts. Before you share a claim, make a decision, or believe a viral post, stop and check the source.

  1. Stop

    Don’t immediately react.

  2. Check

    Find the original source.

  3. Verify

    Look for official or credible documentation.

  4. Decide

    Make your own informed decision.

You don’t owe anyone your vote. You owe yourself enough information to make your own decision.

What URVoteMatters is

A nonpartisan civic information and voter participation resource.

What URVoteMatters is not

It is not affiliated with a political party or candidate and does not tell voters how to vote.

Official information and our guidance

Links marked Official go to government election offices and tools. Everything written on this page is URVoteMatters educational guidance. It is not legal advice and doesn’t replace your election office. When rules matter, follow your state and local election officials.
